# Failure journal

Running list, newest entry at the bottom. Pattern: **we tried X because Y, it failed because Z, so we switched to W.**

| Date | What we tried and why | How it failed / why we dropped it | What we did instead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 14 Nov | Reuse Kryštof's sentiment notebook, because its four architectures already work. | It reports test accuracy only; this course also grades error analysis, the journal and limits. | Reused only its modelling skeleton (MLP, CNN, BiLSTM, DistilBERT); our own evaluation and error analysis. |
| 14 Nov | Google Maps reviews, because the proposal names Maps and e-shops. | The Places API gives at most 5 reviews per place and forbids storing them; scraping breaks Google's terms; dates show as "3 years ago", so the pre-ChatGPT cutoff cannot be checked. | Steam: documented endpoint with exact timestamps and a date filter. Cost: game domain only, thumbs up/down instead of stars. |
| 14 Nov | Steam's `store.steampowered.com/appreviews`, used by most tutorials. | Valve marks it as deprecated; it could stop working before the defence. | `IUserReviewsService/GetAppReviews`, which also has a date filter. |
| 14 Nov | Cleaning checked language and length before the symbol rules. | On a 10-review test, ASCII art was counted as "non-English" and the repeated-character rule never fired, so per-rule counts would be wrong. | Rules reordered (symbols, language, length, duplicates) and re-tested. |
| 14 Nov | Keep reviews of any length, because the proposal focuses on short reviews. | 3,335 of 12,000 (28%) had under 3 words ("good game"); the first extended collection confirmed it (9,360 of 36,000, 26%; 18.6% in the final v3 collection). | Minimum of 3 words, reported as a limit. |
| 14 Nov | 8 well-rated games × 1,500 reviews, chosen for genre variety. | Only 7,940 kept, and we expected too few negative reviews (share not measured before deciding). | 12 games × 3,000, adding Cyberpunk 2077, Battlefield 2042, Fallout 76 and PUBG. |
| 14 Nov | Generate AI reviews with the team's Claude Max and Google AI Pro subscriptions. | These cover the chat apps only; a script needs separate API access. | Checked the API options (resolved 18 Nov, see below). |
| 17 Nov | One date window (1 Jan 2015 – 29 Nov 2022), 3,000 reviews per game. | Steam returns newest first, so the cap was hit within weeks: all reviews from 8 Jul – 29 Nov 2022, 40% from 22–29 Nov (likely the Autumn Sale). | 30-day windows, 3,000 reviews split evenly over them, random pick inside each (seed 42). |
| 17 Nov | Cleaning rule "same character 10+ times", against keyboard mashing and ASCII art. | 17 of 25 sampled removals were genuine reviews ("damnnnnnnnn that player count"). Dropping them makes the human class tidier than it is. | Rule removed; ASCII art is still caught by "mostly symbols". |
| 17 Nov | Rule "under 90% plain Latin letters", against non-English reviews tagged as English. | 19 of 25 sampled removals were correct, but 2 were English in decorative Unicode letters. | Language, word count and duplicate checks run on an NFKC-normalised copy; the stored text is unchanged. |
| 17 Nov | 30-day windows with a random pick inside each. | The pick came from the newest ~100 reviews only: 77% of reviews from the last 3 days of a window (~10% expected; PUBG 98%). | v3: one random 7-day slice per month. Days 21–31 fell from 48.4% to 37.5%, 22–29 Nov 2022 to 0.6%. Days 1–10 still hold 23.7% (~33% expected), reported as a limit. |
| 18 Nov | AI reviews through an LLM API (Gemini free tier, Claude Console). | No API access in the team: Gemini free tier reported as unavailable in the EU, Claude API needs paid credit. Generating by hand in a chat was rejected: no fixed model or settings, reviews influence each other, pairs get mixed up. | Two open models on a Colab GPU: Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct held out as the unseen generator (notebook 02). Limit: small 7B models only. |
| 18 Nov | 100-review Mistral test with prompt v1: "Write a user review of the video game X for the Steam store." | Lengths matched, but the text read like adverts: "Unmatched Fun! Don't Miss It!". A detector would separate it trivially. | Prompt v2: the model is the player, writes in first person, "not like an advertisement". The v1 reviews were deleted and the test repeated. |
| 18 Nov | An AI twin for every cleaned human review (27,691). | ~77 reviews/min on a Colab L4: over 6 h instead of ~2.5, and games stay unbalanced (1,923 PUBG vs 2,444 Fallout 76). | 1,000 human reviews per game (≤ 300 words, seed 42), one AI twin each; the rest kept as a reserve for the false-positive rate. Split 70/15/15 by pair, stratified by game. |
| 18 Nov | Match AI length to the human twin by asking for "about N words". | Full Mistral run: median 29 words vs target 18; for 3–10-word targets it writes 10–20, so length may reveal the class. Also 129 exact duplicates and 1,494 outputs in quotation marks (both removed). | Notebook 02b: off-length reviews regenerated with "at most / at least N words", 4 candidates, closest kept, marked `length_fix`. Plus a length-only baseline and results by length. |
| 18 Nov | Ran the length fix on both generators (11,609 reviews). | Partly worked: median error 7 → 5 words, Mistral median 29 → 23 (target 18); 318 (2.7%) still off. Qwen quirks: 25 drifts into non-Latin script (removed), 8 with Chinese characters, 50 with hashtags, ~6% duplicates (Mistral 1.4%). | Kept; the remaining gap is measured by the length-only baseline (53%). The 8 reviews with Chinese characters are dropped in `scripts/build_dataset.py`. |
| 22 Nov | First Keras run (notebook 04): MLP with three preprocessing variants, CNN, BiLSTM. | 4 of 5 runs learned nothing: 50% accuracy, false-positive rate 99–100%. The training split was ordered (all human, then all AI) and not shuffled. Failed runs kept in `results/04_models/runs_log.csv`. | Training data shuffled before batching, all Keras models retrained. Lesson: check the order of the data, not only its content. |
| 22 Nov | Keep case and punctuation in the TF-IDF MLP, because humans write lowercase "i" and Mistral never does. | Better on Mistral (98.6 → 99.3%, false positives 1.7 → 0.7%) but worse on Qwen (recall 75.4 → 70.6%): the signal is partly Mistral's own habit. Removing stopwords was worst (Qwen recall 63.2%). | Reported as a trade-off, not a winner. Next: a pre-trained model, to see if it transfers better. |
| 23 Nov | DistilBERT (cased), expecting a pre-trained model to generalise to the unseen generator. | Best model, but Qwen recall is only 77.1% (99.6% on Mistral), and it flags 1.12% of real players. More architectures would not answer why. | Stopped adding models; next step is finding out which Qwen reviews are missed and why. |
| 26 Nov | Two explanations we expected for the Qwen gap: (a) Qwen reviews score just below the 0.5 threshold (ROC-AUC 0.97); (b) the model learned "2015–2022 language = human". | (a) Wrong: the misses score ~0 (median 0.000); a threshold for 1% false positives on validation lifts recall only to 78.7%. (b) No sign: the false-positive rate is flat across years. Actual pattern: Mistral never starts with "This game", 41% of missed Qwen reviews do. | Notebook 06 (error analysis from saved predictions, no retraining); the report's error analysis is built around this pattern plus a stress test with small edits. |
