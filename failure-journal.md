| 2026-11-17 | Collected 3,000 reviews per game in one date window (1 Jan 2015 – 29 Nov 2022), expecting reviews from the whole period. | Steam returns the newest reviews first, so the cap of 3,000 was reached within weeks: all kept reviews are from 8 Jul – 29 Nov 2022, 40% of them from the last 8 days (22–29 Nov, likely the Steam Autumn Sale). | Cut the period into 30-day windows and split the 3,000 reviews per game evenly over the windows in which the game has reviews, picked at random inside each window (seed 42). |

| 2026-11-17 | Checked by eye the rule "fewer than 90% plain Latin letters", meant to remove non-English reviews tagged as English. | 19 of 25 sampled reviews were correctly removed (Chinese, Russian, Arabic...), but 2 were English written in decorative Unicode ("Ｔｈｅ ｇａｍｅ＇ｓ ｇｏｔ ｌａｓｅｒｓ...", "𝙰𝚏𝚝𝚎𝚛 𝟽 𝚢𝚎𝚊𝚛𝚜..."). | Language check, word count and duplicate check now use Unicode NFKC normalization; the stored text stays unchanged. |
| 2026-11-17 | Spread the reviews over time with 30-day windows and a random pick inside each window. | Inside a window the collector picks from the newest ~100 reviews only, so 77% of kept reviews come from the last 3 days of their window (about 10% expected if spread evenly; PUBG 98%, Terraria 96%). | 2026-11-18 | Test run of 100 Mistral reviews with prompt v1: "Write a user review of the video game X for the Steam store. | 2026-11-18 | Considered generating an AI twin for every cleaned human review (27,691), to use all the data we collected. | Not worth it: generation runs at about 77 reviews/min on a Colab L4, so all reviews would take over 6 hours instead of about 2.5 (and twice the compute units), training would slow down the same way, and the games would stay unbalanced (1,923 PUBG vs 2,444 Fallout 76). The other 15,691 human reviews stay unused as a reserve test set for the false-positive rate (real players flagged as AI). Planned split: 70/15/15 by pair (a human review and its AI twin always in the same set, to avoid leakage), stratified by game. |
| 2026-11-18 | Matched every AI review to the length of its human twin by asking for "about N words" in the prompt. | Full Mistral run (9,000 generated, 8,870 kept): long reviews follow the target well, but short targets do not. For targets of 3-10 words the models write 10-20 words: they cannot write "Yes, it's a good game". Length alone may therefore partly reveal the class. Also: 129 AI reviews were exact duplicates of another AI review (removed by the duplicate rule) and 1,494 were wrapped in quotation marks despite the instruction (removed by post-processing). | Length fix (notebook `02b_fix_lengths.ipynb`, new file `ai_reviews_clean_lengthfix.csv`; the original file is kept): only the off-length reviews (more than 1.5 × target + 3 words, or fewer than target / 1.5 − 3) are regenerated with a hard limit in the prompt ("at most N words" / "at least N words"), 4 candidates each, keeping the one closest to the target. Originals stay in the raw files; replaced reviews are marked `length_fix = True`. In the modelling step we still add a length-only baseline (classifier on word count) and results by length bucket, to show how much length still gives away. |
| 2026-11-18 | Ran the length fix (notebook 02b) on both generators and checked the result (`ai_reviews_clean_lengthfix.csv`, 11,609 reviews: Mistral 8,843, Qwen 2,766). | Partly worked.
